## Iterative refinement

Iterative refinement is performed after each solve with a direct linear solver. The residuals are computed on the uncondensed (possibly unsymmetric) Newton system, i.e. before the condensation of its condensed blocks (e.g. the bound duals of the primal-dual interior-point method); without condensation, the uncondensed system is the augmented system. The residuals use the factorized matrix (regularization included) without the Schur complement of the condensed blocks. Iterative refinement is not performed with limited-memory quasi-Newton Hessians (L-BFGS, L-SR1), since the augmented matrix then does not contain the full Hessian.

If refinement fails, the linear system scaling is switched on (if available and not yet on, see [Linear system scaling](#linear-system-scaling)); otherwise, the pivoting tolerance of the linear solver (MA27, MA57, MUMPS) is increased (at most once per matrix) and the matrix is refactorized; its inertia is checked again and the regularization increased if needed. The increase of the pivoting tolerance is permanent: all subsequent factorizations use the stricter tolerance, up to a solver-specific maximum (1e-4 for MA27 and MA57, 0.1 for MUMPS, as in IPOPT). If the tolerance cannot be increased and the residual ratio is above `iterative_refinement_residual_ratio_singular`, the matrix is deemed singular and the inertia correction strategy increases the regularization. Second-order corrections never modify the factorization (their refined solution is accepted as is).

Setting `iterative_refinement_max_steps` to 0 disables the whole mechanism (no residual computation, no pivoting tolerance increase, no singularity heuristic). Unlike IPOPT's `max_refinement_steps` (which allows up to `max_refinement_steps` + 1 steps), `iterative_refinement_max_steps` is the exact maximum number of refinement steps.

## Linear system scaling

The matrices of the direct linear solver can be scaled symmetrically (S K S, S a positive diagonal matrix: the inertia is preserved), independently of the linear solver, with `linear_system_scaling` (`ruiz` by default; `none`, `ruiz` or `matching`). The solvers' own scalings (`MA57_use_scaling`, `MUMPS_use_scaling`) are applied on top. Computing a scaling at every factorization is expensive, so by default the scaling is switched on only when the factorizations seem inaccurate or expensive (as IPOPT's `linear_scaling_on_demand`), then kept until the end:
- iterative refinement failed (before the pivoting tolerance is increased);
- optionally, the first factorization of a matrix delayed more than `linear_system_scaling_delayed_pivots_threshold` × dimension pivots (MA57, MA86, MUMPS, SSIDS); the scaling then starts at the next matrix. This trigger is disabled by default: the delayed pivots of KKT matrices are mostly structural (2×2 pivots around the zero (2, 2) block) and do not indicate inaccurate factorizations;
- the inertia correction failed (last resort, before the inertia correction gives up): the inertia correction restarts once with scaling;
- fill growth: the factors of the first factorization of a matrix have more than `linear_system_scaling_fill_growth_threshold` (2 by default; ≤ 1: disabled) times the entries of the sparsest such factorization so far, and at least `linear_system_scaling_fill_growth_minimum_entries` (10⁶ by default) entries (MA57, MA86, MUMPS, SSIDS; MA27 and HiPO do not report it; the least-squares multiplier system, much sparser to factorize, is not monitored). The `matching` scaling is then switched on from the next factorization on (replacing `ruiz`), until the end. At the end of interior-point methods, the barrier terms make the KKT matrices badly scaled, threshold pivoting delays a cascade of pivots and the fill explodes, while the factorizations remain accurate (no other trigger fires). Equilibration does not prevent this; the matching-based scaling does. This trigger is also active with `linear_system_scaling_on_demand = false` (then it replaces `ruiz` by `matching`). It is not available with `linear_system_scaling = none`. Smaller factorizations are cheap even with fill growth, and switching the scaling would only perturb the trajectory: on CUTEst, a 2× growth of small factors (tens to hundreds of thousands of entries) is common and is not the cascade above.

The scaling factors are computed once per matrix: the refactorizations of the same matrix (inertia correction retries, pivoting tolerance increase) and the solves with an existing factorization (second-order corrections, iterative refinement) reuse them, as long as the scaled entries of a refactorized matrix remain bounded (at most 10 in absolute value; the scalings make them at most about 1). Otherwise the scaling is recomputed: the matching-based scaling factors may span tens of orders of magnitude at the end of interior-point methods, and a regularization δ of the inertia correction becomes δ s_i² in the scaled matrix, which would then be badly scaled (the fill of the retries explodes). The exception is a switch in the middle of a matrix (inertia correction failure, fill growth): the factorizations that follow use the new scaling, while the solves with the current factorization keep the scaling it was computed with. Well-behaved problems never trigger the scaling and pay nothing.

`ruiz` is the symmetric Ruiz infinity-norm equilibration (Ruiz 2001; Knight, Ruiz & Uçar 2014).

`matching` is the symmetric scaling from a maximum product matching (Duff & Koster 2001, MC64 job 5; Duff & Pralet 2005), implemented in Uno (no HSL dependency) so that all the linear solvers benefit from it: the permutation that maximizes the product of the absolute values of its entries is computed with a sparse Hungarian algorithm (shortest augmenting paths), and its dual variables yield a scaling with which all the entries of S K S are at most 1 in absolute value and the matched entries are ±1 (up to rounding errors). The column duals of the previous matrix warm-start the next one (same sparsity pattern). For structurally singular matrices, the matching of the principal submatrix of the matched indices scales these indices, and every other index is scaled so that the largest entry of its row is 1.
